# iCIMS — October 2026 Workforce Report: Holiday Hiring Cooldown Pressures Retailers to Move Qualified Candidates Faster

**Source:** https://www.icims.com/company/newsroom/octoberinsights2026/
**Published:** 2026-10-08
**Data:** Proprietary iCIMS platform data from 3.1M+ global users and 691M+ candidate profiles; reflects September 2026 activity vs. September 2025 baseline
**Period:** September 2026 data (reported October 2026)
**Best for:** Holiday hiring strategy, frontline/retail seasonal staffing benchmarks, candidate re-engagement tactics, H2 2026 hiring planning

---

## U.S. Hiring Overview: September 2026

- U.S. hiring fell **7% below the September 2025 baseline** in September 2026
- Job openings stayed **3% above** the September 2025 baseline
- The opening-to-hire gap persists for the fourth consecutive month
- Frontline hiring was above the September 2025 baseline from March through August, then **slipped 8% below baseline** in September

## Frontline & Retail Hiring Trends

- Frontline applications ended September **18% below baseline** — the lowest point of 2026
- Applicants per open role hit the **year-low of 29**, down from 40 at the same point in 2025
- Retail time to fill held steady at **37 days**
- Candidates ages 18–24 made up **40% of retail applicants** in September, up from 37% a year earlier — skewing younger as older applicants exit the pool
- High-volume frontline hiring remained above baseline from March through August before the September decline

## Holiday Hiring Pressure Points

- The shrinking applicant pool means retailers must move qualified candidates faster to avoid losing them to competitors
- iCIMS recommends re-engaging former employees and past applicants to supplement new-applicant flow
- AI-powered candidate re-engagement tools cited as a key lever for speed and reach in a tighter pool

## Key Conclusions

- Frontline hiring has turned negative heading into the holiday season, the first such reversal since early 2026
- The applicant-per-opening ratio (29) at year-low signals reduced candidate supply for high-volume retail roles
- Young candidates (18–24) are growing as a share of retail applicants, but overall application volume is constrained
- The openings-vs-hires gap (openings +3%, hires –7%) means demand remains firm but organizations are failing to convert
- Retailers with AI candidate re-engagement capability and former-employee pipelines have a structural advantage this season
