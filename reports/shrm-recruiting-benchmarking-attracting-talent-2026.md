# SHRM — 2026 Recruiting Executives Benchmarking: Attracting Critical Talent

**Source:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking
**Published:** 2026-06-24
**Data:** 1,268 HR professionals specializing in recruitment; SHRM Voice of Work Research Panel + SHRM Membership; participants required to actively specialize in recruitment; organizations of all sizes across multiple industries in the U.S.
**Period:** Survey conducted December 17, 2025 – January 26, 2026
**Best for:** Executive cost-per-hire benchmarks, recruiter workload norms, recruiting priority mapping, critical talent sourcing strategy, time-to-fill benchmarks by position type

---

## Cost-Per-Hire Benchmarks

| Position Type | 2026 Median | 2025 | 2022 | 2017 |
|--------------|------------|------|------|------|
| Executive positions | **$15,000** | $10,600 | $8,800 | $5,000 |
| Nonexecutive positions | Decreased vs. 2025 | — | — | — |

- Executive cost-per-hire has tripled since 2017, driven by competition for critical talent, increased use of retained search firms, and longer hiring cycles
- Nonexecutive time-to-fill actually **decreased** year-over-year — suggesting process improvements or lower bar thresholds are moving some roles faster

## Recruiter Workload Benchmarks

| Organization Size | Median Requisitions per Recruiter (2026) |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Extra-large organizations | **100 requisitions** per recruiter |
| (Other sizes) | Tracked; reported in full data brief |

- Recruiter workloads have increased significantly, particularly in the largest organizations
- Heavy req loads are concentrated in large employers, amplifying the risk of quality decline when screening high volumes

## Recruiting Priorities: What Executives Are Focused On

- **Top CEO and recruiting executive priority for 2026:** Attracting and recruiting critical talent
- **63%** of organizations say developing a critical talent sourcing strategy is the top priority for 2026
- **31%** of recruiting executives identify recruiting critical talent as their single top priority (above all other concerns)
- Over **2 in 3 organizations (67%+)** reported struggles with hiring for open positions in 2026

## Recruiting Challenges

- Critical talent (technical, leadership, specialized roles) is the primary bottleneck — not volume hiring
- Organizations are investing more per hire at the executive level even as they face budget pressure
- The gap between executive-level hiring investment and nonexecutive hiring speed has widened

## Key Conclusions

- **Executive hiring costs have accelerated dramatically:** At $15,000 median cost-per-hire for executives (3× the 2017 level), organizations are spending more per leader even as the supply remains tight — underscoring the case for internal development and succession planning
- **Nonexecutive roles are becoming more efficient:** The decrease in time-to-fill for nonexecutive positions suggests process automation, AI screening, and hiring bar calibration are paying off at scale
- **Recruiter workloads in large orgs are unsustainable:** Median of 100 requisitions per recruiter in extra-large organizations far exceeds published capacity benchmarks (Ashby: 14 open reqs at any moment), pointing to chronic understaffing
- **Critical talent strategy is the #1 CEO-level priority:** 63% citing it as a top 2026 priority signals a shift from volume hiring to precision hiring for high-impact roles
- **Hiring remains broadly difficult:** 67%+ of organizations report ongoing struggles, confirming that despite some efficiency gains, the talent market remains structurally tight
