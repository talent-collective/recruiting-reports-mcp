# SHRM — 2026 Recruiting Executives Benchmarking: Attracting Critical Talent

**Source:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking/full-data-brief
**Published:** 2026-06 (late June 2026)
**Data:** 4,657 SHRM members surveyed; random sample with stratified oversample of director-level+ respondents
**Period:** Survey conducted November 24, 2025 – January 23, 2026; data not weighted
**Best for:** Cost-per-hire benchmarks by role level, time-to-fill norms, recruiter workload by org size, year-over-year trend comparisons for HR benchmarking

---

## Cost-Per-Hire Benchmarks

| Position Type | 2017 | 2022 | 2025 | 2026 |
|--------------|------|------|------|------|
| Executive | $5,000 | $8,800 | $10,600 | $15,000 |
| Nonexecutive | — | — | $1,200 | $1,300 |

- Median cost-per-hire for executive positions reached $15,000 in 2026 — a 41% increase over 2025 and a 200% increase since 2017
- Nonexecutive median cost-per-hire increased only slightly, from $1,200 to $1,300

## Time-to-Fill Benchmarks

- Median time-to-fill for nonexecutive positions: **39 calendar days** in 2026 (down from 44 days in 2025)
- Time-to-fill is measured from requisition opening to offer acceptance, using calendar days including weekends
- Over 2 in 3 organizations (67%+) reported struggles with hiring for open positions in 2026

## Recruiter Workload by Organization Size

- Extra-large organizations: each recruiter managed a **median of 100 requisitions** in 2026
- This represents a **67% increase** in requisitions per recruiter at extra-large organizations year-over-year
- Approximately 50% of organizations carry a recruiter-to-requisition ratio of ~20 reqs per recruiter; higher loads skew toward larger firms

## Methodology Note

- Data collected from a random sample of active SHRM members, with stratified oversampling of director-level and above
- 4,657 respondents total; individual metric sample sizes vary as not all respondents answered every question
- Median values used throughout to improve cross-period comparability and reduce distortion from outliers
- Diverse representation across industries, sectors, and organization sizes in the U.S.

## Key Conclusions

- Executive hiring costs are accelerating sharply: the $15,000 median cost-per-hire is nearly triple the 2017 baseline, signaling intensified competition for leadership talent and higher use of search firms
- Nonexecutive time-to-fill improved (39 vs. 44 days), suggesting operational streamlining even as hiring difficulty remains high
- Recruiter overload is most severe at large employers: 100 reqs per recruiter at extra-large orgs is unsustainable and likely drives sourcing quality and speed tradeoffs
- Hiring difficulty persists broadly: 2 in 3 organizations still struggle to fill open positions despite a nominally improving labor market
- Trend data spanning 2017–2026 shows cost-per-hire has been on a structural upward trajectory for executive roles, with a particularly steep jump in 2025–2026
