# SHRM — 2026 Recruiting Executives Benchmarking: Attracting Critical Talent

**Source:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/recruiting-benchmarking
**Published:** 2026-06-24
**Data:** 1,268 HR professionals specializing in recruitment; SHRM Voice of Work Research Panel + SHRM Membership; all org sizes, multiple industries, U.S.
**Period:** Survey fielded Dec. 17, 2025 – Jan. 26, 2026
**Best for:** Time-to-fill and cost-per-hire benchmarks, critical talent sourcing strategy priorities, AI/automation adoption expectations in TA

---

## Methodology

SHRM surveyed 1,268 HR professionals whose primary role or significant responsibility is recruitment, drawing from the SHRM Voice of Work Research Panel and SHRM Membership. Respondents represent organizations of all sizes across multiple industries and geographies in the U.S. This benchmarking data brief is a companion to the SHRM 2026 Recruiting Executives Priorities and Perspectives full report (298 heads of recruiting), but is a distinct, larger-sample survey focused on operational benchmarks rather than priorities and perspectives.

---

## Hiring Challenges in 2026

- **67%** (over 2 in 3) organizations report struggles with hiring for open positions in 2026
- **46%** cite labor market competition as a challenge; **16%** say it is their single greatest challenge
- **42%** identify talent shortages as a challenge; **14%** say it is their greatest challenge

---

## Critical Talent Strategy: Top Priority

- **63%** say developing a critical talent sourcing strategy is the top recruiting priority for 2026
- **31%** specifically highlight recruiting critical talent (those with critical skills) as their top priority
- "Critical talent" sourcing is the #1 CEO and recruiting executive priority, above process efficiency and cost reduction

---

## Time-to-Fill Benchmarks

| Position Type | 2026 Median TTF | vs. Prior Year |
|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Nonexecutive | 39 days | ↓ 5 days (from 44 in 2025) |
| Executive | 45 days | Unchanged (from 60 in 2022) |

- Nonexecutive time-to-fill has improved meaningfully; executive hiring timelines remain elevated versus historical norms

---

## Cost-Per-Hire Benchmarks

| Position Type | Median CPH | vs. Prior Year |
|--------------|------------|----------------|
| Nonexecutive | ~$4,700 | Tracking with 2025 |
| Executive | $15,000 | ↑ from $10,600 in 2025 (+41%) |

- Executive hires cost approximately **7x more** than nonexecutive hires on average
- Executive CPH has risen sharply (+41% year-over-year) and is up dramatically from $5,000 reported in 2017

---

## AI and Automation in Recruiting

- **87%** of recruiting leaders expect increased use of AI and automation in general recruiting processes
- **85%** forecast greater adoption of specific AI applications such as chatbots and automated resume screening
- **92%** anticipate generative AI for job descriptions and recruitment content to become more prevalent; **~40%** say "much more prevalent"
- AI is most commonly applied in: **recruiting (27%)**, HR technology (21%), learning & development (17%), employee experience (14%)

---

## AI Risk and Quality Concerns

- **19%** of organizations using AI in hiring report that their tools have overlooked or screened out qualified applicants
- Despite AI adoption, average cost-per-hire and time-to-hire have both increased over the past three years — the period correlating with the initial wave of generative AI adoption

---

## Key Conclusions

- Critical talent sourcing is the #1 recruiting priority in 2026, cited by 63% of organizations — execution on this requires investment in specialized sourcing strategies, not just general recruiting efficiency
- Time-to-fill is improving for nonexecutive roles (39 days, down from 44), suggesting AI screening and automation are compressing early-funnel cycles
- Executive CPH has spiked 41% year-over-year to $15,000 median, signaling increased competition and use of external search firms for leadership roles
- AI adoption in TA is nearly universal in intent (87–92% expecting more), but 19% of current users already report AI screening out qualified candidates — quality guardrails are lagging
- The gap between AI expectations and measured outcomes mirrors the broader HR-technology adoption pattern: fast deployment, slow quality improvement
