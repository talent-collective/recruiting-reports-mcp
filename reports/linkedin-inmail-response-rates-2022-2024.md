# LinkedIn Talent Solutions — InMail Response Rate Studies

Two editions of the same LinkedIn Talent Blog research series, kept in one file because they
are the same measurement with different windows. Both are LinkedIn's own platform data on
recruiter InMail — **not email**. LinkedIn does not publish the underlying dataset.

**Sources**
- 2022 edition — "How to Improve Your InMail Response Rate, According to LinkedIn Data":
  https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-strategy/these-inmails-get-best-response-rates
- 2024 edition — "How InMail Response Rates Compare Across Industries and Functions":
  https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-engagement/how-inmail-response-rates-compare-across-industries-and-functions

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-09. LinkedIn blocks automated scraping; content was retrieved via
fetch-and-extract, so the quotes below are the extractable sentences rather than a full-page
capture. Verify against the live pages before quoting externally.

---

## Methodology

**2022 edition:** tens of millions of InMails from corporate recruiters worldwide,
**May 2021 – April 2022**. Members studied were active users with relatively complete profiles;
staffing firms excluded. Responses counted within a **30-day** window.

**2024 edition (verbatim):** *"This analysis was based on tens of millions of InMails sent by
corporate recruiters around the world between May 2023 through April 2024."* Same exclusions
(active users with relatively complete profiles; staffing firms excluded; 30-day response window).

---

## Message Length — the headline finding, stable across both editions

| InMail length | Response rate vs global average |
|---|---|
| Under 400 characters | **+22%** |
| 400–800 characters | +5% |
| 800–1,200 characters | −6% |
| Over 1,200 characters | **−11%** |

**Distribution of what recruiters actually send:** only **10%** of all InMails are under 400
characters; **46%** exceed 800 characters.

2024 verbatim: *"The shortest InMails — ones of 400 or fewer characters — get responses at a
rate that's 22% higher than the global average."*

---

## Day of Week (2022 edition)

- **Monday** best performing.
- Sunday–Thursday all within ~1% of average.
- **Friday: −4%** below average.
- **Saturday: −8%** below average (worst day).

---

## Personalization / Send Mode (2022 edition)

- InMails sent **individually** see response rates roughly **15% higher** than InMails sent in bulk.

---

## Candidate Signals (2022 edition)

- Candidates surfaced as **"Recommended Matches": 35%** higher acceptance likelihood.
- Candidates with **"Open to Work": 37%** higher response rate than others.

---

## Response Speed (2022 edition)

- **65%** of responders reply within **24 hours**.
- **90%** reply within **one week**.

## Cross-source note (important — do not average these)

- LinkedIn says **shorter is better** (under 400 characters ≈ 50–70 words).
- Pin's 2026 recruiting **email** data says the peak is **150–199 words**, with sub-50-word
  notes the *worst* performing bucket (2.43%).
- These are different channels measured by different owners. The honest reading is that InMail
  rewards brevity and cold email rewards enough substance to justify itself — not that one
  source is wrong. Treat message length as channel-specific and worth testing directly.

Related: Pin's 2026 report independently cites the LinkedIn figure — *"short InMails under 400
characters earn 22% higher response rates than average (LinkedIn, 2024)"* — and reports the same
kind of function-level spread on its own email data.

---

## Key Conclusions

- Message length is the single most reliably reported InMail lever, and the market is on the
  wrong side of it: only 10% of InMails are under the length that wins.
- Individually-sent beats bulk by ~15% — a personalization effect measured on LinkedIn's own
  send-mode flag rather than on message content.
- Candidate state matters as much as copy: Open-to-Work candidates respond 37% more.
- Reply fast or not at all — 90% of the replies that will ever come arrive within a week.
