# KPMG & REC — UK Report on Jobs: November 2026

**Source:** https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/11/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs-november-2026.html
**Published:** November 9, 2026 (covering October 2026 survey data)
**Data:** Monthly questionnaire panel of approximately 400 UK recruitment and employment consultancies; survey period October 12–26, 2026; compiled by S&P Global
**Period:** October 2026 activity (survey fielded Oct 12–26, 2026)

## Headline Labour Market Indicators — October 2026

| Metric | October 2026 | Prior Month | Trend |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Permanent Placements Index | 46.6 | 50.9 (September) | Contraction — first decline in 4 months |
| Temporary Billings | Declined | Rising | First decline since March 2026 |
| Overall Vacancy Levels | Declining | Declining | Softest pace of decline since August 2024 |
| Starting Salaries (permanent) | Rising | Rising | Growth rate strengthened month-on-month |

*Index readings below 50 indicate contraction; above 50 indicate expansion. Compiled by S&P Global.*

## Permanent Placements

- **Permanent Placements Index fell to 46.6** in October from 50.9 in September — the first contraction in four months
- Panellists cited **delayed and reduced client hiring decisions**, driven by rising operating costs, elevated borrowing costs, and broader economic uncertainty
- The Bank of England's interest rate stance and expectations of continued inflation were cited as near-term headwinds
- **Regional breakdown:** The South of England recorded the **sharpest regional fall**; the Midlands also saw a solid decline
- Despite the headline contraction, **6 of 10 monitored job categories** saw increased demand for permanent workers in October
- **Sector leaders:** IT & Computing and Engineering sectors showed the strongest demand growth among permanent placements

## Temporary Staffing

- **Temporary billings fell for the first time since March 2026**, signalling cooling demand for flexible labour
- Despite the decline, temporary pay rates continued to rise, suggesting that the temp pool tightened even as overall demand softened
- Sector divergence was evident: some sectors remained resilient on temporary demand while headline figures turned negative

## Pay and Vacancy Data

- **Starting salaries for permanent roles** saw a stronger rate of increase compared to September, maintaining above-inflation growth
- **Temporary/contract pay rates** continued rising, sustaining recruiter revenue per placement even as volumes compressed
- **Overall vacancy advertising levels** continued to fall, but at the **softest pace since August 2024** — the first meaningful sign of deceleration in the vacancy decline trend

## Recruiter Sentiment and Context

- Recruiters flagged **rising employer cost pressures** as the primary brake on new permanent hiring decisions — specifically, national insurance contribution changes and energy cost pass-throughs
- Demand signals remain bifurcated: white-collar and knowledge-sector roles (IT, Engineering) outperformed, while generalist and service-sector permanent placements contracted more sharply
- The temporary market's first contraction since March reflects a broader employer caution, though flexible talent retention is still being priced at a premium

## Key Conclusions

- **UK permanent hiring stalled in October** after three months of recovery — the Permanent Placements Index reverting to 46.6 (contraction zone) is a meaningful reversal of the Q3 2026 stabilisation trend
- **Cost headwinds are the dominant hiring brake**, not skills shortage or lack of demand — the report singles out employer cost pressures (NI changes, borrowing costs) as the specific mechanism
- **Temp market followed suit** for the first time since March, suggesting cost caution is spreading from permanent to flexible headcount decisions
- **Pay is still rising** for both permanent starters and temps — the talent market remains tight even as placement volumes compress
- **IT and Engineering sectors are resilient** in this environment; TA teams operating in those sectors face a different market dynamic than generalist recruiters
