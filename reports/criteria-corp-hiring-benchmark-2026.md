# Criteria Corp — 2026–2027 Hiring Benchmark Report

**Source:** https://www.criteriacorp.com/research/2026-2027-hiring-benchmark-report
**Published:** 2026-10-06
**Data:** 1,027 U.S. hiring professionals surveyed (largest sample in the series' 9-year history)
**Period:** Survey fielded 2026; covers hiring conditions experienced through 2026
**Best for:** AI adoption in hiring, skills-based screening, Gen Z workplace readiness, hiring difficulty benchmarks

---

## Hiring Difficulty

- **52%** of hiring professionals say hiring is **more challenging** in 2026 vs. last year; only **12%** say it is easier
- Mid-sized companies and the **tech and retail industries** report the most difficulty
- **55%** of hiring professionals say finding high-quality candidates with the right skills is their **single biggest challenge**

## AI in Hiring

- More than **half of hiring professionals now use AI** somewhere in their hiring process
- Most believe AI can improve hiring decisions and deliver strong ROI
- A slim majority also believe **AI will eliminate more jobs than it creates**
- The report frames 2026 as the year AI "broke hiring" by amplifying application volume while reducing signal quality

## Gen Z Workplace Readiness

- Only **16% of hiring professionals believe Gen Z is well prepared** for the workplace
- Employers cite gaps in communication skills, professionalism, and ability to work independently
- Skills gap is the dominant driver of hiring difficulty — not candidate quantity, but candidate quality

## Technology and Assessments

- Skills gaps and AI-inflated application volumes are accelerating adoption of pre-hire assessments and structured screening
- Organizations that use objective assessments report higher confidence in hiring decisions
- Workplace readiness — cognitive ability, attention to detail, reliability — is emerging as the key screening dimension

## Key Conclusions

- Hiring difficulty is high and accelerating: 52% say it got harder, driven by skills gaps not candidate scarcity
- AI in hiring is now mainstream (>50% adoption) but introduces new problems: inflated volume, reduced signal quality
- Gen Z workplace readiness is a growing concern — only 16% of employers see them as prepared
- Pre-hire assessments are gaining traction as the antidote to AI-assisted résumé inflation
- Tech and retail are the hardest-hit sectors for hiring difficulty in 2026
