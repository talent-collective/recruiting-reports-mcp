# Criteria Corp — 2026-2027 Hiring Benchmark Report: AI, Skill Gaps and Workplace Readiness Are Reshaping Hiring

**Source:** https://www.criteriacorp.com/resources
**Published:** 2026-10-06
**Data:** Survey of 1,027 U.S.-based hiring professionals — the largest sample in the report's nine-year history
**Period:** Survey conducted in 2026; reflects 2026-2027 hiring outlook
**Best for:** AI impact on hiring strategy, Gen Z readiness assessment, skills-based hiring rationale, entry-level pipeline planning, hiring difficulty benchmarks

---

## Hiring Difficulty in 2026

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Hiring professionals who say hiring is harder in 2026 | 52% |
| Organizations expecting headcount growth in next year | ~67% |
| Biggest challenge (finding candidates with right skills) | 55% |

- More than half of hiring professionals report hiring is harder in 2026, even as two-thirds of organizations plan to grow headcount — the difficulty is not driven by reduced demand
- Finding high-quality candidates with the right skills is the #1 challenge, cited by 55% of respondents
- The combination of rising demand and rising difficulty creates a structural productivity problem for TA functions

## AI and Entry-Level Hiring

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Hiring professionals who say AI may reduce need for entry-level workers | 37% |
| Hiring professionals who believe AI will eliminate more jobs than it creates | 53% |

- More than 1 in 3 hiring professionals believe AI is already reducing demand for entry-level roles — a structural shift with significant implications for early-career pipelines
- A majority (53%) believe AI will eliminate more jobs than it creates over time, reflecting deep ambivalence about AI's net employment impact
- The entry-level pipeline risk has direct implications for workforce strategy: organizations relying on entry-level hiring to build talent pipelines must re-evaluate their sourcing assumptions

## Gen Z Readiness Gap

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Hiring professionals who believe Gen Z is well prepared for the workplace | 16% |
| Hiring professionals who say new graduates lack skills employers need | ~80% (4 in 5) |

- Only 16% of hiring professionals believe Gen Z is well-prepared for the workplace — a stark preparedness gap
- 4 in 5 hiring professionals report that new graduates don't have the skills their organizations actually need
- Gen Z's self-assessment: fewer than 1 in 4 Gen Z respondents said their own generation is ready for the workforce — an unusual alignment between employer and candidate perceptions

## Skills Gap Landscape

- The skills gap is widening, per Criteria Corp's CEO — not stabilizing
- "Workplace readiness" has emerged as a distinct category alongside traditional skills gaps, with behavioral and professional readiness flagged as failing alongside technical skills
- Hiring professionals are increasingly turning to structured assessments to evaluate skills that degree credentials and work history do not signal reliably

## Key Conclusions

- Hiring is harder in 2026 despite strong demand: 52% of hiring pros say difficulty has increased even as ~67% expect headcount to grow — signaling a structural skills mismatch, not a demand problem
- AI's impact on entry-level hiring is materializing: 37% say it may be reducing demand for entry-level roles, and 53% expect AI to be a net job eliminator — early-career pipelines need strategic review
- The Gen Z readiness gap is documented from both sides: employers (84% say graduates lack needed skills) and Gen Z itself (fewer than 25% believe their generation is workforce-ready)
- Finding candidates with the right skills is the top challenge (55%), ahead of sourcing, compensation, and competition — supporting investment in structured skill assessment over credential screening
- Organizations that shift to skills-based hiring and pre-hire assessment will have a structural advantage as the credential-to-skill correlation continues to weaken
