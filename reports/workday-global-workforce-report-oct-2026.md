# Workday — Global Workforce Report: AI Is Rewriting Jobs More Than It's Cutting Them

## Leadership Perspectives on AI's Workforce Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Business leaders who expect AI to help get more from existing employees | 40% |
| Business leaders who expect AI to reduce headcount | 28% |
| Workers who say they know which skills they need | 79% |
| Workers who say their employer helps them develop those skills | 66% |

- The dominant expectation (40% vs. 28%) is that AI will augment existing staff, not eliminate them
- A 13-point gap between workers knowing what they need (79%) and employer support (66%) signals a training delivery problem more than a skills awareness problem

## AI Skills Demand Trends

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Demand for basic AI skills: change since peak | −25% (peaked January 2026) |
| Demand for hands-on/applied AI skills: growth Sep 2025–Jul 2026 | +51% |

- The AI skills market has matured: basic/foundational AI literacy demand has collapsed while hands-on implementation skills are accelerating
- This signals that employers are moving past "AI awareness" requirements to role-specific AI execution capability

## Internal Mobility and Workforce Stability

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Employers where cross-company role moves fell | 57% |
| Promotions trajectory | Flat |
| Employees who went through reorg/restructuring in past year | ~40% |

- Internal mobility is contracting at a majority of employers—reducing an important alternative to external hiring
- High reorganization rates (~40%) create both churn and opportunity for TA as roles get re-scoped

## Hiring Pipeline Metrics

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Median applicants per filled job | 69 (up from 58 a year earlier) |
| Hiring time | ~60 days (stable YoY) |

- Applicant volume per filled role grew +19% year-over-year (69 vs. 58), intensifying screening pressure
- Hiring time held steady despite heavier applicant load—suggesting screening automation is absorbing volume without lengthening cycles

## Key Conclusions

- **AI is a job-rewriting force, not a headcount-cutting one—according to those with the data.** Leaders who see Workday's workforce numbers (40% expect augmentation vs. 28% reduction) are more sanguine than public perception; this is actionable framing for EVP and candidate communications.
- **Basic AI skills have peaked; applied AI skills are the new battleground.** Demand for foundational AI literacy fell 25% from its January 2026 peak while hands-on AI skills surged 51%—TA teams need to update their skills vocabulary and screener criteria now.
- **Internal mobility is in retreat.** At 57% of employers, internal role moves declined, removing a major lever for talent development and creating more external demand than organizations may have planned for.
- **Applicant volume is up sharply but time-to-fill is stable.** A 19% YoY rise in applicants per filled role (69 vs. 58) without a corresponding increase in time-to-fill suggests AI screening tools are absorbing the volume—but also that more candidates are competing for the same roles.
- **The training gap persists despite awareness.** 79% of workers know what skills they need; only 66% say their employer actively helps. Closing this 13-point gap is both a retention risk and a recruiting brand opportunity.
