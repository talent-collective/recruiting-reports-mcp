# iCIMS — August 2026 Workforce Report: Manufacturing Job Openings Surge 29% as Hiring Stalls

**Source:** https://www.icims.com/company/newsroom/augustinsights2026/
**Published:** 2026-08-12
**Data:** Proprietary iCIMS platform data from 3M+ global platform users and 691M+ candidate profiles
**Period:** July 2026 data (released August 2026)
**Best for:** Manufacturing hiring trends, demand-supply gap analysis, demographic shifts in applicant pools, AI-powered recruiting for high-volume roles

---

## Labor Market Snapshot — July 2026

| Metric | Value | Change |
|--------|-------|--------|
| U.S. job openings (July 2026) | — | +17% vs. July 2025 baseline |
| Application volume | — | +6% |
| Hires | — | Flat |
| Opening-to-hire gap | — | Widest of 2026 so far |

- U.S. openings ended July 17% above the July 2025 baseline — a continued acceleration
- Application volume rose 6%, but hires held flat, producing the year's widest opening-to-hire gap
- Monthly opening growth has been accelerating: from 9% (May) → 19% (June) → 17% sustained (July)

## Manufacturing Sector Deep-Dive

- Manufacturing job openings surged **29% year-over-year** in July 2026
- Despite surging demand, hiring remained stalled — mirroring the national pattern
- Manufacturing time-to-fill held at **33 days**, which is 33% faster than the 49-day iCIMS manufacturing sector benchmark
- The gap between openings and hires is especially acute in manufacturing, where the workforce is aging

## Applicant Demographic Shift

- Candidates under 45 made up **85% of manufacturing applicants** in July 2026, up from 79% a year earlier
- Workers 45 and older still make up nearly **47% of the manufacturing workforce**
- The applicant pool is getting younger while the employed workforce skews significantly older
- This demographic mismatch signals a growing succession risk — experienced workers are not being replaced by experienced applicants

## EMEA Contrast

- EMEA continues to show an oversupply dynamic vs. the U.S. undersupply
- EMEA openings rose but applicants per opening remained elevated — a screening-volume challenge vs. the U.S. sourcing challenge

## Key Conclusions

- Manufacturing is the most acute demand-supply mismatch in the July 2026 labor market: openings up 29% while hiring stalls
- The widest opening-to-hire gap of 2026 reflects employers posting roles but holding back on converting — caution still dominant despite strong demand signals
- A generational demographic cliff is forming in manufacturing: 85% of applicants are under 45, but nearly half the current workforce is 45+, creating a future replacement pipeline risk
- Application volume is growing (+6%) but not converting to hires — quality and speed of screening are the bottlenecks, not pipeline volume
- AI-powered recruiting tools are increasingly critical for high-volume, hard-to-fill roles where manual screening creates delays
