# Cochrane — Methods to Increase Response to Postal and Electronic Questionnaires (MR000008.pub5)

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-09 from the Cochrane Library. DOI 10.1002/14651858.MR000008.pub5.

**Why a survey-methods review is in a recruiting library.** This is the highest grade of
evidence that exists on cold-message mechanics: **758 randomised controlled trials**, pooled,
on the question "what makes a stranger who owes you nothing reply to an unsolicited request?"
No recruiting-specific RCT of comparable rigour exists. Every vendor benchmark in this library
is observational; this is not. The domain differs (research questionnaires, not job outreach)
and that limit must be stated whenever a figure from here is used — but the *direction* of
effects here is the closest thing to ground truth cold outreach has.

Results are **odds ratios (OR)** with 95% confidence intervals, random-effects model. `I2` is
the heterogeneity statistic — high `I2` means trial results disagreed a lot and the pooled
number is softer than it looks.

---

## Methodology (verbatim)

> **Search methods:** "We searched 14 electronic databases up to December 2021 and manually
> searched the reference lists of relevant trials and reviews. We contacted the authors of all
> trials or reviews to ask about unpublished trials…"
>
> **Selection criteria:** "Randomised trials of methods to increase response to postal or
> electronic questionnaires."
>
> **Data collection and analysis:** "For each strategy, we estimated pooled odds ratios (OR)
> and 95% confidence intervals (CI) in a random‐effects model. We assessed evidence for
> selection bias using Egger's weighted regression method and Begg's rank correlation test and
> funnel plot. We assessed heterogeneity amongst trial odds ratios using a Chi2 test and
> quantified the degree of inconsistency between trial results using the I2 statistic."

Scope of included populations (verbatim): *"from patients, doctors, university students, and
professors, to marketing managers, accountants, and grocery store managers."*

## ELECTRONIC QUESTIONNAIRES — 88 trials, 30+ strategies (the relevant half)

### Odds of response TRIPLED

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Using a **brief letter** rather than a detailed letter | **3.26** (1.79 to 5.94) |
| Including a **picture in an email** | **3.05** (1.84 to 5.06), I2 = 19% |

### Odds of response almost DOUBLED

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Monetary incentives | 1.88 (1.31 to 2.71), I2 = 79% |
| A more interesting topic | 1.85 (1.52 to 2.26) |

### Odds increased by about half

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Non-monetary incentives | 1.60 (1.25 to 2.05) |
| **Shorter e-questionnaires** | 1.51 (1.06 to 2.16), I2 = 94% |
| A more interesting e-questionnaire | 1.85 (1.52 to 2.26) |

### Odds increased by about a third

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Offering survey results as an incentive | 1.36 (1.16 to 1.59) |
| Using a white background | 1.31 (1.10 to 1.56) |
| **Stressing the benefits to society of response** | 1.38 (1.07 to 1.78), I2 = 41% |

### Smaller but positive

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **Personalised e-questionnaires** | **1.24** (1.17 to 1.32), I2 = 41% |
| **Using a simple header** | 1.23 (1.03 to 1.48) |
| **Giving a deadline** | 1.18 (1.03 to 1.34) |
| Giving a longer time estimate for completion | 1.25 (0.96 to 1.64) |

### Odds of response were REDUCED

| Strategy | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **"Survey" mentioned in the e-mail subject** | **0.81** (0.67 to 0.97) |
| Email/e-questionnaire from a **male investigator, or including a male signature** | **0.55** (0.38 to 0.80) |
| Using university sponsorship | 0.84 (0.69 to 1.01) |

### Channel comparisons

| Comparison | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Postal questionnaire vs e-questionnaire | **2.33** (2.25 to 2.42), I2 = 98% |
| Offering a choice of response mode (electronic or postal) vs electronic only | 1.76 (1.67 to 1.85), I2 = 97% |
| E-questionnaire by computer rather than by smartphone | 1.62 (1.36 to 1.94) |

## Authors' Conclusions (verbatim)

> "Researchers using postal and electronic questionnaires can increase response using the
> strategies shown to be effective in this Cochrane review."

Plain-language key messages (verbatim):

> "Response to questionnaires can be increased by contacting people before they are sent a
> questionnaire;
> Response to questionnaires can be increased by making questionnaires, letters, and emails
> more personal, and preferably kept short;
> Response to questionnaires can be increased by giving an incentive, for example, a small
> amount of money, or a non‐monetary incentive such as a pen."

## What this transfers to recruiting outreach

- **Personalisation is real but modest** — OR 1.24 on electronic, i.e. ~a quarter more odds of
  reply, not the 2x that vendor observational data reports. The vendor numbers are inflated by
  who bothers to personalise; this is the randomised estimate.
- **Brief beats detailed by a lot** (OR 3.26). The largest single electronic effect in the
  review is "send a short letter rather than a long one."
- **A deadline works** (OR 1.18) and **an explicit obligation-to-respond frame works** on postal
  (OR 1.61). Both are cheap and neither is standard in recruiting copy.
- **Naming the transactional thing in the subject line hurts** — "Survey" in the subject was
  OR 0.81. The recruiting analogue ("Job opportunity", "Hiring", "Recruiting") is untested and
  is the single most obvious A/B to run.
- **Sender identity moves response independently of content** — a male signature was OR 0.55.
  Sender is a lever, not a constant.
- **Pre-contact before the ask works** (OR 1.36 postal). The analogue is a LinkedIn view or
  connection before the email — which is roughly what Pin's multi-channel result shows.
- **Do not quote these as recruiting figures.** They are survey-invitation figures. Cite the
  domain every time.
