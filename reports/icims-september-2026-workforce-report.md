# iCIMS — September 2026 Workforce Report: Workers Are Teaching Themselves AI Skills Faster Than Employers Train Them

**Source:** https://www.icims.com/company/newsroom/septemberinsights2026/
**Published:** 2026-09-10
**Data:** Proprietary iCIMS platform data from 3M+ global platform users and 691M+ candidate profiles; supplemented by survey of 1,000 U.S. adults commissioned by iCIMS
**Period:** August 2026 data (compared to August 2025 baseline); survey conducted in 2026
**Best for:** AI skills gap analysis, employer training investment ROI, AI-powered screening strategy, EMEA vs. U.S. hiring pace comparison, time-to-fill benchmarks

---

## U.S. Labor Market Snapshot — August 2026

| Metric | Value | vs. August 2025 Baseline |
|--------|-------|--------------------------|
| U.S. job openings | — | +13% |
| Time to fill | 40 days | — |
| Applicants per opening | 30 | — |
| Month-over-month growth (openings) | +1% | (down from +7% June, +4% July) |

- Job openings ended August 13% above the August 2025 baseline, but growth is decelerating sharply
- Hiring is slowing even faster than opening growth is cooling — the labor market is tightening from both sides
- Applicants per opening fell to 30, leaving employers less room for delays in screening, interviewing, and offers
- Time to fill reached 40 days, signaling that conversion speed matters more as candidate pools thin

## AI Skills Gap: Workers Leading, Employers Lagging

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| U.S. workers who self-taught AI skills (2026) | 30% (up from 22% a year ago) |
| Workers receiving employer-provided AI training | ~1 in 6 (~17%) — static |
| Job seekers who developed AI skills in last 6 months | 47% |
| Workers who would find AI training more attractive | 42% |
| Workers who would accept lower pay for AI training | 14% |

- Workers are teaching themselves AI at nearly double the rate of employer-provided training
- The share of self-taught AI-skilled workers rose from 22% to 30% in a single year while employer training remained flat
- Despite widespread AI tool familiarity, 61% said their proficiency is limited to general-purpose tools (not specialized AI capabilities employers are hiring for)
- 60% of respondents felt prepared to adapt to AI at work — but preparation and specialized capability are diverging

## AI in the Job Market

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| AI-related job postings as % of U.S. hiring demand | 4% |
| AI-related job postings as % of U.K. hiring demand | 2.7% |
| AI-related job postings as % of France hiring demand | 1.2% |

- AI-specific roles remain a small absolute share of total hiring, but are growing and disproportionately attracting interest
- The gap between AI demand and AI supply is already compressing TA capacity at specialized roles

## Implications for Recruiting and Screening

- As applicants per opening shrinks (30 in August vs. higher earlier in 2026), the cost of slow screening rises
- Employer AI training is an emerging EVP lever: 42% of candidates weigh it and 14% would trade compensation for it
- AI-powered screening becomes more necessary as general-purpose AI skills among candidates grow but specialized readiness doesn't

## Key Conclusions

- U.S. job openings are still up 13% vs. baseline, but month-over-month growth slowed to +1% in August — the market is decelerating
- Workers are outpacing employers on AI skill acquisition: 30% are self-teaching (up from 22% in a year) while employer training is static at ~1 in 6
- 61% of workers report AI proficiency limited to general-purpose tools — a surface-level familiarity that doesn't match the specialized AI skills employers are hiring for
- AI training has become a measurable EVP differentiator: 42% of job seekers favor employers offering it; 14% would accept lower pay for it
- With applicants per opening at 30 and time to fill at 40 days, TA teams need faster screening infrastructure — AI-powered screening is both a tactical and strategic necessity
- EMEA and U.S. hiring are both slowing, even as openings outpace hires — the convergence signals a broader cooling in the global hiring cycle
