# Pin — Recruiting Outreach Benchmarks 2026: The Full Data Report

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-09 from https://www.pin.com/blog/recruiting-outreach-benchmark-report/
(published 2026-07-08, modified 2026-07-21). Pin is an AI recruiting/sourcing platform.

**Why this report matters:** it is the most granular *recruiting-specific* outreach dataset
published with its methodology in the open (no gate, no form). Where Gem's comparable report
is gated and LinkedIn's covers InMail only, this one publishes per-step, per-role, per-hour
cuts for cold email AND LinkedIn under one measurement definition.

---

## Methodology (verbatim)

> "This report quantifies outreach sent through Pin between June 1, 2025 and May 31, 2026:
> 4,000,000+ messages from 1,500+ recruiting organizations to 1,500,000+ candidates. Pin's
> customer base spans 2,000+ organizations and 20,000+ users across in-house talent teams and
> agencies, so the sample is representative of both segments.
>
> A reply means the candidate responded in-thread to the original message. Reply rates use a
> delivered-basis denominator that excludes bounced messages, and internal Pin accounts and
> test organizations were removed prior to analysis. Channel figures cover email and LinkedIn
> messages, the two channels with end-to-end reply tracking; LinkedIn connection requests,
> manual SMS, and phone calls were excluded because acceptance and offline responses can't be
> tracked with the same reliability. Send-time analysis uses U.S. Eastern Time as the
> recruiter-local proxy, and role families derive from each candidate's primary job title.
> Because reply attribution requires owning both the send and the receive loop, channels where
> responses occur off-platform were excluded from the headline figures rather than estimated.
> Quarterly trend data extends back to mid-2024 for additional context."

## Reply Rate by Channel

| Channel | Reply rate (delivered basis) |
|---|---|
| Automated email | **4.96%** |
| Recruiter-written one-off email | **6.31%** |
| LinkedIn message | **17.08%** |

- LinkedIn is a **3.4x differential over automated email**.
- Verbatim: *"That LinkedIn advantage is durable rather than a blip: in every quarter of the
  measured year, LinkedIn messages out-replied automated email by 3-4x, holding between 14%
  and 19% while email stayed near 5%."*
- Verbatim: *"LinkedIn message volume in the dataset multiplied more than fiftyfold over the
  measurement period."*
- Their stated bar: *"A good recruiter email response rate in 2026 is 5% or higher… Anything
  above 8% on email puts a team near the top of the market."*

## Sequence Step Decay — Where Replies Actually Come From

| Step | Reply rate | Share of total replies | Cumulative share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message 1 | 5.52% | 43.8% | 43.8% |
| Message 2 | 5.40% | 33.7% | 77.5% |
| Message 3 | 4.21% | 15.7% | **93.2%** |
| Message 4 | 3.34% | 4.5% | 97.7% |
| Message 5+ | 2.93% | 2.3% | 100% |

- Recommendation verbatim: *"Recruiters should send 3 to 4 messages per sequence, because the
  first three touches capture 93.2% of all replies a sequence will generate and a fourth touch
  lifts that to 97.7%."*
- Their surprise, verbatim: *"We anticipated the first message would dominate and follow-ups
  would decay rapidly. Instead, the second touch replied at 5.40%, nearly matching the first
  message's 5.52%, making it essentially complimentary pipeline for anyone who bothers to send it."*
- *"a sequence that stops at one message leaves 56% of its potential replies unclaimed."*

**Disagreement with Gem, stated in the report:** *"Gem found the first email captures about 58%
of replies with follow-ups adding 42%… In Pin's data, the first message earns a smaller share
(43.8%), meaning follow-ups are doing even more of the work for recruiters now than the older
public data suggested."*

## Reply Speed

- Median time from send to reply: **4 hours**
- Within 24 hours: **74.8%**
- Within 7 days: **93.9%**
- Practical rule given: *"If 48 hours pass in silence the next touch is due."*

## Send Hour (US Eastern)

| Window | Email reply rate |
|---|---|
| Midnight–5am ET | **6.67%** |
| 3–5am ET | **7.24%** |
| Midday | 4.81% |
| Evening | 4.70% |

- Verbatim: *"the improvement held across 237 different organizations, so it isn't one night-owl
  team distorting the data. Candidates process their inboxes first thing in the morning, and the
  messages already positioned on top win the attention competition before the workday buries them."*
- Caveat stated: *"Candidate time zones vary, so treat Eastern as a proxy rather than a law."*

**Opens vs replies warning (verbatim):** *"Gem's research crowned Monday (61.6%) and Sunday
(61.4%) the strongest send days, but those figures measured open rates, not replies… A Sunday
email gets opened in a quiet inbox but answered never, while a Wednesday email gets opened
quickly and answered the same morning. Benchmark the metric you're actually compensated on."*

## Reply Rate by Candidate Role Family

| Role family | Email reply rate |
|---|---|
| Product management | **9.94%** |
| Marketing & comms | 8.57% |
| Design & creative | 8.19% |
| Operations & logistics | 7.67% |
| Executive & leadership | 7.33% |
| HR & recruiting | 6.24% |
| Project & program mgmt | 4.97% |
| Sales & business dev | 4.69% |
| Engineering & data | 4.64% |
| Legal & compliance | 4.09% |
| Healthcare & medical | **2.81%** |

- Spread is **3.5x** top to bottom.
- Verbatim: *"A 4.6% reply rate on an engineering search is on-benchmark, while the identical
  figure on a product-management search indicates the messaging needs recalibrating."*
- Healthcare detail: *"professionals opened 51.8% of recruiting emails yet replied at just
  2.81%"* — i.e. they read and decline, so the failure is the message, not the subject line.

**Companion finding (separate Pin analysis, 940,000+ contacted candidates):** candidates
currently between roles replied *"74% more often than candidates showing no activity signal."*
Within an identical role and sequence the lift was still **34%**.

## Message Formatting

**Subject line length**

| Length | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| 5–6 words | **5.35%** |
| 1–2 words | 4.64% |
| 10+ words | 4.43% |

**Body length**

| Length | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| 150–199 words | **5.46%** |
| 300+ words | 4.55% |
| Under 50 words | **2.43%** |

- Verbatim: *"the 300-plus-word essay was the single most common email in the entire dataset,
  yet the 150-199 word message replied best. Most recruiters are composing twice as much as the
  data rewards."*

**Personalization**

- With candidate's first name: **5.13%** · without: **2.61%** (nearly double)
- Verbatim: *"98% of messages in the dataset already personalized, so the baseline has risen;
  the differentiation now comes from personalizing beyond the name field, referencing the
  candidate's actual work."*

## Multi-Channel — Biggest Lever, and the Caveat

| Sequence shape | Multi-channel (email + LinkedIn) | Email only |
|---|---|---|
| Two-step | **45.76%** | 19.73% |
| Three-step | **22.74%** | 5.77% (3.9x) |

**The caveat is printed in the report and must travel with the number:**

> "One caveat keeps this analysis honest: recruiters tend to add a LinkedIn touch for the
> candidates they want most, so part of the differential reflects recruiter effort and
> prioritization, not the channel alone." … "Association, not pure causation."

Their defence of it anyway: *"Even discounted for that selection effect, the size and
consistency of the gap across sequence lengths makes channel mix the single biggest controllable
variable in the data. It outweighs send day, subject line, and message length combined."*

## Key Conclusions

- Recruiting cold email lands near **5%** reply; LinkedIn near **17%**; the two together is the
  largest single lever in the dataset, with an acknowledged selection-effect caveat.
- **Stop at 3–4 touches.** 93.2% of all replies are already in by touch three.
- The message shape that wins is a **5–6 word subject line over a 150–199 word body** — notably
  *longer* than what sales cold-email research recommends (the 30 Minutes to President's Club /
  Gong analysis of 85M+ sales emails says under 100 words and under 4 subject-line words; not a
  report in this collection). The disagreement is real, both sides are observational, and it is
  a live A/B question for recruiting specifically.
- **Role family moves reply rate 3.5x**, so a single company-wide reply-rate target is a bad KPI.
- Measure replies, not opens — the two metrics crown different send days.
