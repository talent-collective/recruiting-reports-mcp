# Criteria Corp — Hiring Benchmark Report 2026-2027: AI, Skills Gaps, and Workplace Readiness Reshape Hiring

**Source:** https://www.criteriacorp.com/research/2026-2027-hiring-benchmark-report
**Published:** 2026-10-06
**Data:** Survey of 1,027 U.S.-based hiring professionals across a wide range of industries, company sizes, and roles
**Period:** 2026 survey data (ninth annual Hiring Benchmark Report)
**Best for:** AI misrepresentation in hiring, Gen Z workplace readiness benchmarks, skills gap quantification, AI adoption rates in TA, entry-level hiring trends

---

## Overview

Criteria Corp's ninth annual Hiring Benchmark Report is based on 1,027 U.S. hiring professionals — the largest sample to date in the series. It documents three converging pressures reshaping hiring in 2026: AI-enabled candidate misrepresentation, a widening skills gap, and employer skepticism about Gen Z workplace readiness.

## Hiring Difficulty and Confidence

| Finding | Stat |
|---------|------|
| Say hiring is harder in 2026 | 52% |
| Say hiring is easier | 12% |
| Cite finding high-quality candidates as biggest challenge | 55% |
| Expect their organization to grow headcount in next year | ~65% |

- **52%** of hiring professionals say hiring is harder in 2026 — even as a majority expect their own headcount to grow
- Difficulty and growth expectations are simultaneously elevated, indicating a skills-mismatch problem rather than a demand problem

## AI and Candidate Misrepresentation

| Finding | Stat |
|---------|------|
| Dealing with candidates using AI to misrepresent themselves | 40% (4 in 10) |
| Among those, can reliably detect AI misrepresentation | <67% (more than a third cannot) |
| Now use AI in some part of hiring (hiring professionals) | 56% |
| Use AI in hiring, tech sector | 87% |
| Say AI may be reducing need for entry-level hires | 37% |

- **4 in 10** hiring professionals are actively dealing with candidates who use AI to misrepresent their qualifications
- More than a third of those professionals say they cannot reliably detect it — detection capability has not kept pace with misrepresentation capability
- **56%** of hiring professionals now use AI in some part of hiring (up from ~25% in the 2024-2025 edition)
- Tech sector leads AI adoption in hiring at **87%**
- **37%** say AI may be reducing the need for entry-level hires — a structural demand signal

## Gen Z Workplace Readiness

| Finding | Stat |
|---------|------|
| Hiring professionals who think Gen Z is well prepared | 16% |
| Hiring professionals who say Gen Z is "somewhat ready" | ~50% |
| Hiring professionals who say Gen Z is "not ready at all" | ~33% |
| Gen Z respondents who think their generation is prepared | 22% |
| Hiring professionals who say new graduates lack needed skills | 80% (4 in 5) |

- Only **16%** of hiring professionals believe Gen Z is well prepared for the workplace
- Strikingly, **even Gen Z respondents are skeptical**: only 22% say their own generation is adequately prepared
- **4 in 5** hiring professionals say new graduates don't have the skills employers actually need
- Work ethic was the most-cited specific skills gap for new graduates, at **67%**

## Skills Gap Context

- **55%** of hiring professionals cite finding high-quality candidates with the right skills as their single biggest challenge
- The skills gap is structural, not cyclical: difficulty persists even as hiring volume expectations remain positive
- AI-related skill verification is an emerging gap within the gap: employers need to screen for AI proficiency but lack standardized tools

## Key Conclusions

- The 2026 hiring market is characterized by simultaneous difficulty and optimism: 52% say hiring is harder, but ~65% expect to grow headcount — the tension is skills mismatch, not demand shortfall
- AI misrepresentation is a live problem: 40% of hiring professionals encounter it and more than a third cannot reliably detect it, creating selection risk that pre-hire assessments are needed to address
- Gen Z readiness skepticism is unusually bipartisan — employers (84%) and Gen Z themselves (78%) largely agree that the generation is not fully workplace-ready, with work ethic as the most-cited gap
- AI adoption in hiring is accelerating rapidly (56% overall, 87% in tech), creating both efficiency gains and new risks around misrepresentation and entry-level displacement
- 37% of hiring professionals believe AI is already reducing the need for entry-level roles — a signal that early-career pipelines may face structural compression over the next hiring cycle
