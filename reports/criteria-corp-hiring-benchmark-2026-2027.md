# Criteria Corp — 2026–2027 Hiring Benchmark Report: AI, Skill Gaps, and Workplace Readiness

**Source:** https://www.criteriacorp.com/research/2026-2027-hiring-benchmark-report
**Published:** 2026-10-06
**Data:** 1,027 U.S.-based hiring professionals surveyed across industries, company sizes, and roles (ninth annual edition)
**Period:** 2026 survey; reflects hiring conditions and employer sentiment
**Best for:** AI misrepresentation in hiring, Gen Z workplace readiness benchmarks, skill gap quantification, AI adoption rates in screening and assessments

---

## Hiring Difficulty and Market Conditions

- **52%** of hiring professionals say hiring is harder in 2026; only **12%** say it is easier
- **55%** identify finding high-quality candidates with the right skills as their single biggest challenge
- Nearly **two-thirds** expect their organizations to grow headcount in the next year despite the difficulty
- Hiring difficulty is highest in technical roles, which also see the most AI disruption to the candidate pool

## AI in Candidate Behavior and Misrepresentation

- **4 in 10** hiring professionals are struggling with candidates using AI to misrepresent their qualifications or experience
- More than **one-third** of employers cannot reliably detect AI-assisted misrepresentation in applications
- Résumé inflation via AI is widening the gap between application quality and actual candidate capability
- This is driving renewed interest in structured assessments and skills-based screening to surface genuine signal

## AI Adoption in Hiring Processes

- **56%** of hiring organizations have adopted AI in at least one part of their process
- Tech sector leads at **87%** AI adoption; other sectors trail significantly
- **Screening** is the most common use case at **46%**, followed by assessments and interview scheduling at **35%** each
- **37%** of hiring professionals say AI may be reducing their need to hire entry-level workers (jumps to **65%** in tech)

## Gen Z Workplace Readiness

- Only **16%** of hiring professionals believe Gen Z is well-prepared for the workplace
- **4 in 5** believe new graduates lack the skills employers need
- **Work ethic** is the top cited gap (67%), ahead of technical and communication skills
- The readiness perception gap is driving both earlier assessment in the process and investment in structured onboarding

## Key Conclusions

- AI-assisted misrepresentation has emerged as a top hiring challenge: 40% of employers are struggling with it and over a third can't reliably detect it
- Skill gaps — not compensation or sourcing — are now the primary blocker to quality hires for the majority of employers
- Gen Z workforce readiness perceptions are at a low: only 16% of hiring pros rate the cohort as prepared, though this reflects employer perception, not a direct measure of performance
- AI adoption in hiring is uneven by sector: tech at 87%, while other sectors cluster around 50-60%
- The market for structured assessments and skills-based screening is expanding as résumé signal degrades
