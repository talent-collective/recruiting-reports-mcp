# Deloitte — Global Gen Z and Millennial Survey 2026: Progress on Their Own Terms

**Source:** https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/issues/work/genz-millennial-survey.html
**Published:** 2026-05-13
**Data:** 22,595 respondents — 14,384 Gen Zs (born Jan 1995–Dec 2007) and 8,211 millennials (born Jan 1983–Dec 1994) across 44 countries: North America, Latin America, Western Europe, Eastern Europe, Middle East, Africa, Asia-Pacific
**Period:** Survey conducted November 24, 2025 – January 15, 2026 (online, self-complete)
**Best for:** Employer value proposition design, understanding what Gen Z/millennial candidates actually want, AI-at-work expectations, career aspiration benchmarks for offer and retention conversations

---

## Career Goals and Progression Preferences

Gen Z and millennials are explicitly prioritizing stability and fit over speed. Rapid promotions are a minority preference:

| Career growth preference | Gen Z | Millennials |
|---|---|---|
| Steady, gradual growth | 44% | 45% |
| Fast-paced progression / rapid promotions | 25% | 21% |
| Willing to move laterally or step back to find the right role | 21% | 20% |

- Only **6%** of Gen Z and millennials say achieving a leadership position is their primary career goal
- Despite low leadership aspiration as a *primary* goal, **76%** of Gen Z and **67%** of millennials say they are interested in pursuing senior leadership roles at *some point* in their career
- **~40%** have rejected employers or job assignments that conflicted with their personal ethics or beliefs

## Why Gen Z and Millennials Avoid Leadership Roles

The most commonly cited barriers to pursuing leadership:

| Barrier | Gen Z | Millennials |
|---|---|---|
| Stress and burnout | 50% | 49% |
| Excessive responsibility | 50% | 48% |
| Work/life balance concerns | 41% | 46% |

## Mental Health and Stress

Overall mental health is trending upward year-over-year, but a significant portion report chronic stress:

- **52%** of Gen Z and **58%** of millennials rate mental wellbeing as good or extremely good
- **47%** of Gen Z and **41%** of millennials rate mental health as fair or poor
- **40%** of Gen Z and **34%** of millennials say they feel stressed or anxious all or most of the time

## Financial Wellbeing and Housing Impact

Cost of living remains the #1 concern for the fifth consecutive year:

- **55%** of Gen Z and **52%** of millennials have delayed major life decisions (marriage, family, starting a business, further education) because of their financial situation
- Financial optimism is improving: **53%** of Gen Z (up from 49% in 2025) and **45%** of millennials (up from 41% in 2025) expect their personal financial situation to improve in the next 12 months
- **61%** of Gen Z and **64%** of millennials say housing availability or affordability has a direct impact on their career decisions and where they can work

## AI at Work

Both generations see AI as a career accelerant, not a threat — a notable contrast with employer-side anxiety:

- **74%** of Gen Z and **74%** of millennials report using AI to some extent in their day-to-day work
- The dominant framing: AI as an accelerant to learning and career growth, especially for entry-level workers
- This contrasts with employer-side AI anxiety; Gen Z and millennials largely self-report comfort with and adoption of AI tools

## Employer Expectations and Purpose

- Nearly all respondents say having a sense of purpose at work is important to job satisfaction and wellbeing
- **~40%** have rejected an employer or assignment that conflicted with their personal ethics or beliefs

## Key Conclusions

- **Career path framing matters in recruiting.** Only 6% want to climb fast; most want stability, skill-building, and gradual progress. Job descriptions and offer conversations that emphasize "career ladder" may actively repel the majority of Gen Z/millennial talent.
- **Leadership pipeline challenge is structural, not motivational.** 76% of Gen Z *want* to lead eventually — they're just deterred by burnout risk and work/life concerns. EVPs that address sustainability and flexibility reduce the friction.
- **Ethics rejection is real and common.** ~40% have turned down a job or assignment for ethics reasons — TA teams need to treat employer brand and social stance as screening criteria, not just marketing.
- **Housing = remote work pressure.** 61–64% link where they can live to where they can work. Salary and remote-work policy aren't separate levers; they're the same lever.
- **AI adoption gap is narrowing from the candidate side.** At 74% AI usage, Gen Z and millennials are as AI-native as most employers — "candidates who cheat with AI" framing misrepresents this generation's actual relationship with these tools.
