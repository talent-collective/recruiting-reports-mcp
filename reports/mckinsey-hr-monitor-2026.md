# McKinsey — HR Monitor 2026: A Turning Point for the People Function

**Source:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/hr-monitor
**Published:** 2026-06-14
**Data:** ~1,300 HR professionals + 5,500 employees across 10 countries (Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Spain, UK, USA, China)
**Period:** Survey conducted Q1 2026; findings cover full 2025–2026 HR landscape
**Best for:** Benchmarking HR function maturity, AI-in-HR adoption vs. impact gaps, global retention and training norms

---

## Methodology

McKinsey's People & Organizational Performance Practice surveyed approximately 1,300 HR professionals and 5,500 employees across ten countries. Primary focus is Europe (8 EU/UK markets), with comparative data from the U.S. and China. The 44-page report benchmarks five core people-function domains: workforce planning, talent acquisition, employee development, performance management, and employee experience. Now in its second year, the HR Monitor is designed as an annual benchmark series.

---

## Workforce Planning

- Only **11%** of organizations adopt a long-term strategic perspective on workforce planning; the rest focus on short-term headcount
- Most organizations remain reactive—planning capacity rather than capability
- HR is increasingly expected to move beyond operational headcount planning to scenario-based skills forecasting

---

## Talent Acquisition

- Global offer acceptance rates rose **+3 percentage points** year-over-year, indicating an employer-favoring labor market
- Overall hiring success (filled roles meeting quality criteria) up **+4 percentage points** globally
- U.S. average offer acceptance rate: **56%** — notably lower than the 82% benchmark in Gem's 2026 U.S.-only data, reflecting the McKinsey report's European-weighted global sample
- Long hiring cycles persist and raise risk of losing top candidates despite improved acceptance rates
- McKinsey recommends building proactive pipelines via passive candidate relationship management, strong referral programs, and reengaging prior "runner-up" candidates

---

## Employee Development and Training

- **24%** of employees report zero training participation in the past year
- Employees report an average of **3.4 training days/year** — HR professionals estimate nearly twice as much at **6.2 days**
- Large perception gap between HR and employees on development investment signals systemic measurement or delivery failure

---

## Performance Management and Feedback

- **>50%** of employees receive performance feedback once per year or not at all
- **20%** of employees had no career development meeting or feedback session in the past year
- Infrequent feedback cycles correlate with declining employee engagement and attrition risk

---

## Retention and Compensation

- **52%** of employees say remuneration and additional benefits is the top retention factor — up sharply from **24%** the prior year
- Compensation has more than doubled in salience as a retention driver in one year, reflecting cost-of-living pressures and competing offers
- Non-monetary factors (career growth, culture, flexibility) remain important but are now clearly secondary to pay

---

## AI Adoption in HR

- **80%** of companies have deployed AI in at least one HR domain
- But only **1 in 5 (20%)** has fundamentally transformed how HR work gets done through AI
- AI adoption is progressing slowly — **0–6 percentage point increases** in operational use per domain, depending on function
- Deployments are largely concentrated in administrative areas; strategic AI use remains rare
- Most organizations remain in pilot mode; few have scaled AI beyond experimentation

---

## HR–Employee Perception Gap

- Across all five HR domains, HR professionals consistently rate their organization's effectiveness higher than employees do
- The gap is largest in development and feedback, where HR believes investment is adequate but employees report a different reality
- McKinsey frames this as the core challenge: HR thinks it is doing more than employees experience

---

## Key Conclusions

- The HR function is at an inflection point: AI deployment is widespread (80%) but transformational impact is rare (20%), creating a significant gap between hype and changed work
- Compensation has surged to the top retention factor (52%, up from 24%), signaling that EVP strategies built on non-monetary benefits are losing ground
- Training and feedback delivery are significantly overestimated by HR relative to actual employee experience — a systemic blind spot that compounds attrition risk
- Workforce planning remains predominantly short-term headcount-focused; only 11% of organizations have shifted to strategic capability planning
- Global hiring is improving (OAR +3pp, hiring success +4pp) but U.S. acceptance rates (56%) trail the broader market, and long hiring cycles remain a pipeline risk
